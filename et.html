Ethiopia was never colonized, and it shows: its own calendar, its own alphabet, rock-hewn churches carved a thousand years ago, and a coffee culture that gave the world the drink in the first place.

Tastes of Ethiopia

Injera with wat
Spongy sourdough flatbread served with an array of spiced meat or vegetable stews, eaten communally by hand.
Kitfo
Minced raw or lightly warmed beef seasoned with spiced butter and chili, a delicacy often reserved for special occasions.
Coffee ceremony
Fresh-roasted and brewed coffee served in three rounds, a genuine daily ritual rather than a tourist show.
Tej
A traditional honey wine, often homemade and served in a distinctive round flask-shaped glass.

Top attractions

Rock-Hewn Churches of Lalibela
Eleven medieval churches carved directly downward out of solid volcanic rock in the 12th and 13th centuries, each one excavated as a single monolithic block rather than built up from stone. Bet Giyorgis, carved in the shape of a cross and sunk into a deep pit, is the most photographed of the group. They remain active places of worship, with pilgrims and priests moving through the same spaces daily, not a preserved ruin.
Simien Mountains National Park
A UNESCO-listed range of jagged peaks, deep escarpments, and high-altitude plateaus in Ethiopia's north, home to the gelada, a grass-eating monkey found nowhere else on Earth, often encountered in large troops along the trekking routes. Multi-day treks with camping are the standard way to experience the park, passing dramatic cliff-edge viewpoints along the way. Ras Dashen, Ethiopia's highest peak, sits within the range for those wanting a longer trek.
Aksum's obelisks and ruins
The ancient capital of the Aksumite Empire, once a major trading power, marked today by towering carved granite obelisks, royal tombs, and the church said to house the Ark of the Covenant according to Ethiopian Orthodox tradition. Access to the Ark itself is restricted to a single guardian monk, but the surrounding church complex and archaeological sites are open to visitors. It's one of the key stops on Ethiopia's northern historical circuit alongside Lalibela and Gondar.
Gondar's Royal Enclosure
A walled compound of stone castles built by a succession of Ethiopian emperors starting in the 17th century, earning Gondar the nickname 'Africa's Camelot.' The mix of Portuguese, Indian, and local architectural styles reflects Ethiopia's long history of selective outside contact. It's an easy add-on to a Lalibela or Simien Mountains itinerary, both reachable within a day's travel.
Ethiopian coffee ceremony
Ethiopia is widely credited as coffee's birthplace, and the traditional ceremony, green beans roasted, ground, and brewed fresh in front of guests over the course of an hour or more, remains a genuine daily social ritual rather than a tourist performance. It's typically offered three times through a single sitting, each round considered a distinct blessing. Experiencing one, whether in a home, market stall, or restaurant, is one of the most authentic things a visitor can do in Ethiopia.
Danakil Depression
One of the hottest, lowest, and most alien landscapes on Earth, home to active volcano Erta Ale's lava lake, sulfur-yellow hydrothermal fields at Dallol, and vast white salt flats worked by camel caravans. It sits below sea level and regularly ranks among the most extreme inhabited environments on the planet. Trips require a organized tour with guides familiar with the remote, occasionally politically sensitive border region.

Suggested itinerary

10 days

10 days covers the classic northern historical circuit — Lalibela, Gondar, the Simiens, and Aksum — without rushing. 5–6 days works for Lalibela and Gondar alone; 14+ lets you add the Danakil Depression or the southern Omo Valley in more depth.

1. Days 1–2
Addis Ababa
The capital's museums, markets, and a first coffee ceremony before heading north.
Bole International Airport is ~15 min from central Addis Ababa
Where to eat: Try injera with a mix of wats (stews), Ethiopia's communal, shared-plate national meal.
2. Days 3–4
Lalibela
The rock-hewn churches, still active places of worship carved a thousand years ago.
~1h15 domestic flight from Addis Ababa
Cost: Church complex entry costs around USD 50 for foreign visitors, covering all eleven churches.
3. Days 5–6
Gondar & Simien Mountains
Royal castles, then a multi-day trek into the highlands to see the gelada monkeys.
~1h domestic flight from Lalibela to Gondar, then a short drive to the parkBook ahead
Cost: A multi-day Simien trek with guide, scout, and camping gear runs €50–90 per person per day.
4. Days 7–10
Aksum & the northern circuit
Ancient obelisks, royal tombs, and the church tradition says holds the Ark of the Covenant.
~45 min domestic flight from Gondar
Where to eat: Try tej, Ethiopian honey wine, often served in a traditional flask-shaped glass.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a visa for Ethiopia?
Most nationalities need an e-Visa, applied for online before arrival — apply with a few days' buffer before your flight.
Which airport should I fly into?
Addis Ababa's Bole International (ADD), a major hub with strong connections across Africa and internationally.
Is Ethiopia's calendar really different?
Yes — Ethiopia uses its own calendar, roughly seven to eight years behind the Gregorian calendar, with a 13th month, so dates on local materials may look unfamiliar.
Is Ethiopia safe to visit right now?
The main northern historical circuit is generally safe and well-traveled; check current advisories for southern and border regions before finalizing a route, as conditions vary by area.
