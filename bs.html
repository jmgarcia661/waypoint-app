Tastes of Bahamas

Conch salad
Raw conch diced with citrus, peppers, and onion, made fresh to order at beachside stalls.
Cracked conch
Conch pounded tender, breaded, and fried, a Bahamian comfort-food staple.
Peas and rice
Pigeon peas and rice cooked with thyme and pork, the classic Bahamian side dish.
Rum cake
A dense, boozy cake soaked in Bahamian rum, sold across the islands as a classic souvenir.

Top attractions

Exuma & the swimming pigs
A chain of over 300 small islands and cays famous for the swimming pigs of Big Major Cay, who paddle out to greet arriving boats expecting food. Beyond the pigs, Exuma is known for Thunderball Grotto, a cave used in a James Bond film, and some of the most saturated turquoise water in the Bahamas. Most visitors see it on a day-boat tour from George Town or Nassau.
Nassau's Straw Market & Queen's Staircase
The Bahamian capital's old town centers on the Straw Market, packed with handwoven baskets, hats, and souvenirs, and the Queen's Staircase, 65 limestone steps carved by enslaved laborers in the 18th century leading up to Fort Fincastle. Together they give a sense of Nassau's colonial history alongside its present-day cruise-port energy. It's an easy walk between the two from the harbor.
Pink Sands Beach, Harbour Island
A three-mile stretch of genuinely pink-tinted sand on a small, low-key island reached by boat from Eleuthera, its color coming from crushed coral and shell fragments mixed into the sand. Harbour Island's small town of Dunmore Town adds pastel colonial cottages and golf-cart-only streets to the appeal. It's a favorite with travelers wanting a quieter, more boutique alternative to Nassau.
Andros barrier reef
The largest and least developed of the major Bahamian islands, fronted by the third-largest barrier reef in the world and riddled with inland 'blue holes,' deep vertical caves that drop straight down into the earth. It's a serious draw for divers and anglers rather than a beach-resort crowd. The island's slow pace and thick pine forest interior feel a world away from Nassau's cruise-ship bustle.
Conch shacks
Roadside and beachside shacks serving fresh conch, the Bahamas' signature shellfish, cracked and prepared to order as salad, fritters, or cracked conch (breaded and fried). Nassau's Arawak Cay, nicknamed 'Fish Fry,' is the best-known cluster of these stalls, lively with music most evenings. It's the most authentic, unfussy way to eat in the islands.
Blue Lagoon Island
A small private island a short boat ride from Nassau, popular for its calm beaches, dolphin encounter program, and easy day-trip logistics for cruise passengers and Nassau-based travelers alike. It's a straightforward, low-effort beach day compared to venturing out to the Family Islands. Hammocks and beach loungers line the shore, with food and drink available on-site.

Suggested itinerary

6 days

6 days allows Nassau plus two Family Islands without too much inter-island flight time. 3–4 days suits Nassau and one island; 8–10 lets you add Andros or the Abacos in more depth.

1. Days 1–2
Nassau & Paradise Island
The Straw Market, Queen's Staircase, and an easy beach day at Blue Lagoon Island.
Lynden Pindling International Airport is ~20 min from central Nassau
Where to eat: Try conch salad, made fresh to order, at Arawak Cay's 'Fish Fry' stalls.
2. Days 3–4
Exuma
Swimming pigs, Thunderball Grotto, and island-hopping by boat.

Harbour Island
Pink sand beaches and a slower, boutique pace to close the trip.
~35 min domestic flight to Eleuthera, then a short water taxi
Where to eat: Try cracked conch, breaded and fried, at a beachfront restaurant on Harbour Island.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a visa for the Bahamas?
Not for EU, UK, US, and most Commonwealth passport holders for short tourist stays — always confirm current rules before booking.
Which airport should I fly into?
Nassau's Lynden Pindling International (NAS) for most itineraries, with onward domestic flights to the Family Islands.
Is it easy to see the swimming pigs?
Yes — day-boat tours run regularly from both Nassau and George Town, Exuma, and are the standard way most visitors see them.
Is the Bahamas expensive?
Yes, generally — most food and goods are imported, and prices run noticeably higher than mainland US or Europe, especially on the smaller islands.
