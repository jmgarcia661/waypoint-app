Best time to visit

Romania trades the crowds of Western Europe for fortified medieval towns, castles with genuine Dracula lore, and Carpathian villages where horse carts still outnumber cars.

Tastes of Romania

Sarmale
Cabbage leaves rolled around seasoned minced meat and rice, simmered slowly — a classic Romanian comfort dish.
Ciorba
A family of sour soups, often made with vegetables, meat, or tripe, soured with vinegar or fermented bran.
Mici
Small grilled rolls of minced, garlicky meat, a staple at Romanian barbecues and street food stalls.
Papanasi
A fried or boiled doughnut made from cheese-enriched dough, topped with sour cream and fruit jam.

Top attractions

Bran Castle
A dramatic hilltop fortress widely marketed as 'Dracula's Castle', its association with Bram Stoker's novel looser in fact than in legend, but the atmosphere delivers regardless. Turrets and narrow stone stairways lead through furnished rooms once used by Romanian royalty. It's easily combined with nearby Brasov and Rasnov Fortress on a single day out from either base.
Brasov & the old town
A Saxon-founded city ringed by forested hills, with a pastel-colored old town square anchored by the Gothic Black Church. A cable car climbs Tampa Mountain for a panoramic view over the terracotta rooftops below. It functions as the natural base for exploring both Bran Castle and the surrounding Transylvanian countryside.
Sighisoara
A UNESCO-listed fortified medieval town, and the birthplace of Vlad the Impaler, the historical figure who partly inspired Dracula. Cobbled lanes climb past pastel townhouses to a covered wooden staircase leading up to a hilltop church and cemetery. It's one of the few inhabited medieval citadels left in Europe, not just a preserved museum piece.
Peles Castle
A lavish Neo-Renaissance royal palace built for Romania's first king in the late 19th century, its interiors dense with carved wood, stained glass, and armor collections. It sits in the mountain town of Sinaia, an easy stop between Bucharest and Brasov. Interior visits require a guided tour and often sell out at peak times in summer.
Transfagarasan Highway
A dramatic mountain road built in the 1970s that switchbacks over the Carpathians, once called the world's best driving road by Top Gear. It passes glacial Balea Lake near its highest point and is open only in summer, closing under snow the rest of the year. Driving it is as much the point of a visit as any single destination along the way.
Bucharest's Old Town & Palace of Parliament
The capital pairs a lively, bar-filled old town with one of the heaviest buildings on Earth: the vast Palace of the Parliament, built under Ceausescu and second in size only to the Pentagon among the world's administrative buildings. Guided tours cover a fraction of its thousand-plus rooms. Bucharest's grand, faded Belle Epoque boulevards earned it the old nickname 'Little Paris.'

Suggested itinerary

8 days

8 days covers Bucharest and a proper Transylvania loop without rushing the castles. 5 works for Brasov and Bran alone; 10–12 lets you add Sibiu, the Transfagarasan drive, and a slower pace through the Saxon villages.

1. Days 1–2
Bucharest
The capital's old town, grand boulevards, and the enormous Palace of the Parliament.
Henri Coanda Airport is ~40 min from central Bucharest
Where to eat: Try sarmale, cabbage rolls stuffed with meat and rice, Romania's classic comfort dish.
2. Days 3–4
Brasov & Bran
A fairy-tale old town, a cable car viewpoint, and 'Dracula's Castle' nearby.
~2.5h drive or train from Bucharest
Cost: Bran Castle entry costs around RON 60 (~€12); the Tampa Mountain cable car adds a similar amount.
Where to eat: Try a bowl of ciorba, Romania's sour soup tradition, before exploring Brasov's main square.
3. Days 5–6
Sighisoara & Transylvanian villages
A medieval citadel and a scenic drive through fortified Saxon villages.
~1.5h drive from Brasov
Where to eat: Try papanasi, a fried doughnut topped with sour cream and jam, a beloved dessert across Transylvania.
4. Days 7–8
Sibiu & the Transfagarasan
A charming Saxon city and, in summer, a drive over the dramatic mountain highway.
~1.5h drive from Sighisoara; the Transfagarasan is open roughly June–October
Where to eat: Try a plate of mici, small grilled minced-meat rolls, a staple of Romanian barbecues.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a visa for Romania?
Not for EU citizens, and not for UK, US, and many other passport holders for short tourist stays — always confirm current rules before booking.
Which airport should I fly into?
Bucharest's Henri Coanda International (OTP) for most itineraries, or Cluj-Napoca (CLJ) if focusing purely on Transylvania.
Is Bran Castle really Dracula's Castle?
The historical connection to Bram Stoker's novel is loose at best, but the castle and its marketing lean fully into the legend, and it's worth visiting for the setting alone.
When is the Transfagarasan Highway open?
Roughly June to October, weather permitting — it closes under snow and ice for the rest of the year.
