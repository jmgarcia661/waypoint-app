Lithuania is the Baltics at their most underrated: a baroque old town that survived Soviet occupation intact, a lakeside island castle, and a wind-carved dune coast unlike anywhere else in Europe.

Tastes of Lithuania

Cepelinai
Large potato dumplings stuffed with minced meat or cheese, served with sour cream and bacon bits — Lithuania's national dish.
Saltibarsciai
A vivid pink cold soup made from beets and kefir, a refreshing summer staple.
Kibinai
Savory hand pies filled with meat, brought to Trakai by the Karaite community centuries ago.
Sakotis
A distinctive spit cake with a tree-like, spiky exterior, traditionally served at weddings and celebrations.

Top attractions

Vilnius Old Town
A UNESCO-listed old town packed with baroque churches, cobbled lanes, and courtyards, one of the largest and best-preserved historic centers in Eastern Europe. The self-declared 'Republic of Uzupis', a bohemian artists' quarter with its own tongue-in-cheek constitution, sits just across the river. Gediminas' Tower on a hill above town offers the best panoramic view of the rooftops below.
Trakai Island Castle
A red-brick Gothic castle rebuilt on a small island in Lake Galve, connected to the mainland by wooden footbridges, and one of the most photographed sights in the Baltics. It was once the seat of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania and now houses a history museum inside its restored halls. The lakeside town around it is known for kibinai, a savory pastry brought by the Karaite community centuries ago.
Curonian Spit
A narrow, UNESCO-listed sand-dune peninsula stretching along the Baltic coast, shared with Russia's Kaliningrad region, with pine forests on one side and towering shifting dunes on the other. The Hill of Witches near Juodkrante is lined with folk-art wooden sculptures along a forest trail. Nida, the main town, has a distinctive fishing-village charm with colorful wooden houses.
Hill of Crosses
A small hill near Siauliai covered with an estimated hundred thousand crosses, crucifixes, and rosaries, left by pilgrims over more than a century as an act of faith and quiet resistance during Soviet rule. Authorities bulldozed it repeatedly in the 1960s and 1970s, and it was rebuilt each time. It remains an active pilgrimage site and one of Lithuania's most striking, unusual sights.
Kaunas Old Town
Lithuania's second city and interwar capital, known for its exceptional concentration of Art Deco and Bauhaus-influenced architecture from the 1920s and 30s. The Ninth Fort on the city's edge documents a darker chapter as a Nazi-era mass execution site, offering a sobering counterpoint to the old town's cafes and church spires. It's an easy day trip or overnight stop between Vilnius and the coast.
Uzupis
A small, self-declared 'republic' within Vilnius, a former rundown district reinvented by artists in the 1990s into a quirky bohemian enclave. Its tongue-in-cheek constitution, posted on a wall in dozens of languages, includes lines like 'everyone has the right to be happy' and 'a dog has the right to be a dog.' Galleries, riverside cafes, and street art fill its narrow lanes.

Suggested itinerary

5 days

5 days covers Vilnius, Trakai, and a coastal detour to the Curonian Spit at a relaxed pace. 3 works for Vilnius and Trakai alone; 7–8 lets you add the Hill of Crosses and more time on the coast.

1. Days 1–3
Vilnius & Trakai
The baroque old town, the bohemian Uzupis district, and a day trip to the island castle.
Vilnius Airport is ~7 km from the city center; Trakai is a ~30 min drive or bus ride
Where to eat: Try cepelinai, Lithuania's beloved potato-dumpling dish, at a traditional restaurant in the old town.
2. Day 4
Kaunas
Art Deco architecture and interwar history on a relaxed day trip from Vilnius.
~1h15 drive or train from Vilnius
Where to eat: Try a bowl of saltibarsciai, Lithuania's vivid pink cold beet soup, especially refreshing in summer.
3. Day 5
Curonian Spit
Shifting sand dunes and a fishing-village coastline to close the trip.
~3.5h drive from Kaunas to Klaipeda, then a short ferry to the Spit
Cost: A ferry across to the Curonian Spit costs a few euros each way for foot passengers.
Where to eat: Try smoked fish sold straight from smokehouses along Nida's harbor.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a visa for Lithuania?
Not for EU citizens, and not for UK, US, and many other passport holders for short Schengen-area stays.
Which airport should I fly into?
Vilnius International Airport (VNO) for most itineraries, or Kaunas Airport (KUN) for budget-airline connections.
Is Lithuania part of the Schengen Area?
Yes, and it uses the euro, making it straightforward to combine with a wider European trip.
How do you get to the Curonian Spit?
By driving or taking a bus to Klaipeda, then a short ferry crossing to reach the Spit itself.
