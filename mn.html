Mongolia is one of the last places where nomadic life is still everyday reality, not a museum piece — endless grassland, Gobi Desert dunes, and a night sky uninterrupted by city light for hundreds of kilometers.

Tastes of Mongolia

Buuz
Steamed dumplings filled with seasoned mutton, a Mongolian staple especially common at festivals.
Khorkhog
Mutton slow-cooked with hot stones inside a sealed pot, a traditional celebratory dish.
Airag
Fermented mare's milk, a mildly alcoholic drink central to nomadic hospitality and summer gatherings.
Boortsog
Small fried dough biscuits, a common tea-time snack across the country.

Top attractions

Gobi Desert
A vast desert of rock, sparse grassland, and the towering Khongoryn Els sand dunes, which reach up to 300 meters high and are known locally as the 'singing dunes' for the sound the wind makes moving over them. Camel treks and 4x4 crossings are the standard way to explore, usually combined with nights in ger camps under exceptionally dark, star-filled skies. Dinosaur fossil sites, including the famous Flaming Cliffs, are scattered across the region too.
Khövsgöl Lake
One of the world's clearest freshwater lakes, ringed by forested mountains in Mongolia's far north, sometimes called the 'Blue Pearl' of the country. Local Tsaatan reindeer herders live in the surrounding taiga, and horseback or hiking trips around the lake are the main way to experience the region. It's a long journey from Ulaanbaatar, rewarding travelers with more time to spare.
Ger camp stays
Staying in a traditional round felt tent, still the everyday home for many nomadic and semi-nomadic Mongolian families, is one of the most immersive experiences the country offers. Camps range from simple, family-run setups to more comfortable tourist-oriented versions with proper beds and shared showers. Meals often include fresh dairy products and, in ger stays with a host family, a genuine glimpse into daily herding life.
Terelj National Park
A dramatic landscape of granite rock formations, pine forest, and open valley just a couple of hours from Ulaanbaatar, making it the easiest taste of the Mongolian countryside for travelers short on time. Turtle Rock, a boulder naturally shaped like its namesake, is the park's best-known formation. It's a popular spot for a one- or two-night ger stay without committing to a longer expedition.
Naadam Festival
Mongolia's biggest annual festival, held every July, centered on the 'three manly games': wrestling, horse racing, and archery, held in venues across the country but most spectacularly in Ulaanbaatar's main stadium. Colorful traditional dress, throat singing, and a genuinely festive national atmosphere surround the competitions. Timing a trip around Naadam adds a cultural layer well beyond the usual steppe and desert scenery.
Gandantegchinlen Monastery
Ulaanbaatar's largest working Buddhist monastery, home to a 26-meter gilded statue of Avalokiteshvara and a community of resident monks who survived decades of Soviet-era religious suppression. Morning prayer sessions are open to respectful visitors and offer a genuine, unstaged glimpse into Mongolian Buddhist practice. It's an easy stop within the capital before heading out to the countryside.

Suggested itinerary

9 days

9 days allows a real taste of both the Gobi and the north without excessive travel fatigue, since distances are vast. 4–5 days suits a Terelj and Gobi-only trip; 12–14 lets you add western Mongolia's mountains in more depth.

1. Days 1–2
Ulaanbaatar & Terelj National Park
The capital's monasteries and museums, plus an easy taste of the countryside nearby.
Chinggis Khaan International Airport is ~20 min from central Ulaanbaatar
Where to eat: Try buuz, steamed mutton dumplings, a Mongolian staple especially common during festivals.
2. Days 3–6
Gobi Desert
Singing sand dunes, camel treks, and nights in ger camps under dark skies.
~1h domestic flight to Dalanzadgad, or a multi-day overland 4x4 routeBook ahead
Cost: A multi-day Gobi tour with driver, guide, and ger accommodation runs €70–130 per person per day.
3. Days 7–9
Khövsgöl Lake
Clear northern lake, horseback riding, and reindeer-herder communities to close the trip.
~1h domestic flight to Mörön, then a short drive to the lake
Where to eat: Try fresh dairy products offered by host families at nomadic ger camps around the lake.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a visa for Mongolia?
Not for many nationalities for short tourist stays — check current rules for your passport before booking.
Which airport should I fly into?
Ulaanbaatar's Chinggis Khaan International Airport (ULN), the country's main gateway.
Do I need a guide to travel around Mongolia?
Not strictly required, but strongly recommended outside Ulaanbaatar — roads are often unpaved and unmarked, and distances are vast.
When is the best time to visit?
June to September, when the weather is warmest and most ger camps and tour operators are running; winters are extremely cold, especially outside the capital.
