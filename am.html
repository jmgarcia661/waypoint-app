Best time to visit

Armenia was the first country to adopt Christianity as a state religion, and it shows in monasteries perched on cliff edges across a landscape watched over by Mount Ararat, just over the closed border in Turkey.

Tastes of Armenia

Khorovats
Armenian barbecue, usually pork, lamb, or vegetables grilled over an open fire and wrapped in lavash bread.
Lavash
A thin, soft flatbread central to Armenian cuisine, recognized by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage.
Dolma
Grape or cabbage leaves stuffed with spiced minced meat and rice, a staple at Armenian gatherings.
Gata
A sweet, layered pastry with a buttery filling, traditionally baked for holidays and celebrations.

Top attractions

Geghard Monastery
A UNESCO-listed monastery partially carved directly into the cliff face of a mountain gorge, founded in the 4th century around a sacred spring said to flow from inside the rock. Some chambers are entirely hewn from solid stone, including a mausoleum with remarkable natural acoustics still used for choral performances. It sits within a short drive of Yerevan, often combined with the temple at Garni.
Tatev Monastery
A 9th-century monastery perched dramatically on a rocky plateau above the Vorotan River gorge in Armenia's south, reached by the Wings of Tatev, one of the world's longest reversible cable cars. The ride alone, stretching over five and a half kilometers above the gorge, is worth the trip regardless of the monastery at the end. It's a full-day excursion from Yerevan given the distance, or a stop on a longer southern Armenia route.
Yerevan & Mount Ararat views
Armenia's capital sits within clear view of Mount Ararat, the biblical peak central to Armenian identity despite lying just across the closed border in Turkey. The Cascade, a giant tiered stone stairway doubling as an outdoor sculpture garden and modern art museum, climbs a hillside with the mountain as backdrop. Republic Square's pink volcanic tuff buildings and a lively cafe culture give the city an unexpectedly relaxed, Mediterranean-adjacent feel.
Lake Sevan
One of the largest high-altitude lakes in the world, sitting at nearly 1,900 meters and ringed by mountains, a popular summer escape from Yerevan's heat. Sevanavank Monastery occupies a small peninsula jutting into the lake, reached by a short climb from the shore. Beach clubs and fish restaurants specializing in the lake's own trout species line the western shore in summer.
Armenian wine country (Areni)
Home to what's widely considered the world's oldest known winery, a roughly 6,100-year-old wine production site discovered in the nearby Areni-1 cave complex. Modern wineries in the same valley now produce wine from indigenous grape varieties found almost nowhere else, part of a small but growing wine tourism scene. It pairs naturally with a visit to Noravank Monastery, dramatically set in a narrow red-rock canyon nearby.
Khor Virap Monastery
A monastery built atop the pit where Armenia's patron saint, Gregory the Illuminator, was reportedly imprisoned for over a decade before converting the king to Christianity. It offers what's often considered the single best view of Mount Ararat anywhere in the country, the mountain rising directly behind the monastery walls. It's an easy half-day trip from Yerevan and one of the most photographed sights in Armenia.

Suggested itinerary

7 days

7 days covers Yerevan and the classic southern monastery route — Khor Virap, Geghard, Areni, Tatev — plus Lake Sevan. 3–4 days works for Yerevan and nearby monasteries alone; 10 lets you add northern Armenia's Debed Canyon monasteries in more depth.

1. Days 1–2
Yerevan
The Cascade, Republic Square, and the city's cafe culture with Ararat views.
Zvartnots International Airport is ~10 min from central Yerevan
Where to eat: Try khorovats, Armenian barbecue, at a traditional restaurant with fresh lavash bread.
2. Day 3
Khor Virap & Geghard
Mount Ararat's best viewpoint and a monastery carved into a mountain gorge.
~45 min drive from Yerevan to Khor Virap, then on to Geghard
Where to eat: Try gata, a sweet Armenian pastry, sold at roadside stalls near the monasteries.
3. Day 4
Areni & Noravank
Armenia's ancient wine country and a monastery in a red-rock canyon.
~2h drive from Yerevan
4. Days 5–6
Tatev
A cliffside monastery reached by one of the world's longest cable cars.
~4h drive from Yerevan; often paired with an overnight in the south
Cost: The Wings of Tatev cable car costs about AMD 7,000 (~€17) round trip.
5. Day 7
Lake Sevan
A high-altitude lake and lakeside monastery to close the trip.
~1h drive from Yerevan on the way back from the south
Where to eat: Try Sevan trout (ishkhan), a local specialty found only in this lake.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a visa for Armenia?
Not for EU, UK, US, and many other passport holders for short tourist stays — always confirm current rules before booking.
Which airport should I fly into?
Yerevan's Zvartnots International Airport (EVN), the country's main gateway.
Can I see Mount Ararat up close?
You can see it clearly from Yerevan and especially from Khor Virap Monastery, but the mountain itself lies across the closed border in Turkey and isn't accessible from Armenia.
Is Armenia easy to combine with Georgia?
Yes — the land border between Armenia and Georgia is open and well-traveled, and many visitors combine both countries in a single Caucasus trip.
