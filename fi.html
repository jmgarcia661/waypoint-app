Best time to visit

Finland swings between two extremes travelers love: endless bright summer nights around thousands of lakes, and genuine Arctic winters with the northern lights and reindeer to match.

Tastes of Finland

Salmon soup (lohikeitto)
A creamy soup of salmon, potatoes, and dill, a comforting Finnish classic.
Karelian pasties
Thin rye pastries filled with rice or potato, traditionally topped with an egg-butter spread.
Reindeer (poronkäristys)
Sautéed reindeer meat, a Lapland specialty usually served with mashed potato and lingonberry jam.
Cinnamon buns (korvapuusti)
Finland's take on the cinnamon roll, a fixture of coffee culture nationwide.

Top attractions

Helsinki Design District & Suomenlinna
Helsinki pairs a compact, design-forward city center with Suomenlinna, a UNESCO-listed sea fortress spread across a cluster of islands a short ferry ride from the harbor. The fortress's grassy ramparts and old cannons make for an easy half-day walk, with museums and a working shipyard along the way. The city center itself is known for clean Nordic design shops and a striking mix of Art Nouveau and modernist architecture.
Finnish sauna culture
Finland is the birthplace of the sauna, and with roughly one for every two residents, it remains a genuine daily ritual rather than a spa novelty. Public saunas like Helsinki's Löyly, right on the waterfront, let visitors experience it properly, often followed by a plunge into the sea even in winter. UNESCO recognized Finnish sauna culture as intangible heritage, underlining just how central it is to national identity.
Lapland & the Northern Lights
Finland's Arctic north, where the aurora borealis is visible on clear nights for much of the winter, especially around Rovaniemi and further north near Saariselkä. Glass igloos and snow hotels let visitors watch the lights from a heated bed, while husky and reindeer sled rides round out the classic Lapland experience. In summer, the same region offers near-endless daylight instead.
Lakeland (Saimaa)
Finland's largest lake system, a maze of over 10,000 interconnected lakes and islands in the country's southeast, popular for boating, fishing, and lakeside sauna cabins. Saimaa is also home to one of the world's rarest seals, the Saimaa ringed seal, found nowhere else on Earth. Savonlinna, on the lake's edge, hosts a well-known opera festival each July inside a medieval castle.
Rovaniemi & Santa Claus Village
Officially marketed as the hometown of Santa Claus, this attraction sits directly on the Arctic Circle just outside Rovaniemi, with a line painted on the ground marking the exact latitude. It's a genuinely popular family destination, especially around Christmas, but also works as a jumping-off point for husky safaris and aurora viewing year-round. Rovaniemi itself is Lapland's practical hub, with direct flights from Helsinki.
Temppeliaukio Church
A striking church carved directly into solid granite bedrock in central Helsinki, its rough rock walls topped with a copper dome and skylight ring that floods the interior with natural light. Excellent acoustics make it a popular concert venue alongside its role as a working Lutheran church. It's one of the most distinctive pieces of modern architecture in the city and a quick stop from the center.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a visa for Finland?
Not for EU citizens, and not for UK, US, and many other passport holders for short Schengen-area stays.
Which airport should I fly into?
Helsinki-Vantaa (HEL) for most itineraries, or Rovaniemi (RVN) if flying direct to Lapland.
When is the best time to see the northern lights?
Roughly late August through March in Lapland, on clear, dark nights away from city lights — there's no guarantee on any single night.
Is Finnish sauna etiquette different from what I'm used to?
Often, yes — many Finnish saunas are used nude and single-sex by default, though mixed and swimsuit-friendly public options exist too; check the specific venue's rules.
