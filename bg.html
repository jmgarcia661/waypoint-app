Best time to visit

Bulgaria is Europe's least-crowded bargain: Roman ruins under a modern city, monasteries painted floor to ceiling, and Black Sea beaches at a fraction of the cost of its neighbors.

Tastes of Bulgaria

Shopska salad
A fresh salad of tomatoes, cucumbers, peppers, and onion topped with grated white cheese.
Banitsa
A flaky pastry filled with cheese and egg, eaten for breakfast across the country.
Kavarma
A slow-cooked stew of meat and vegetables, often served bubbling in a clay pot.
Rakia
A strong fruit brandy, usually plum or grape, central to Bulgarian hospitality and toasts.

Top attractions

Rila Monastery
Bulgaria's largest and most important Orthodox monastery, its striped arches and vividly frescoed courtyard tucked into a forested mountain valley two hours from Sofia. Founded in the 10th century and rebuilt after a 19th-century fire, it remains an active monastery and a national symbol of cultural survival through Ottoman rule. Most visitors come as a day trip, though the setting rewards those who linger past the tour-bus crowds.
Plovdiv Old Town & Roman Theatre
A beautifully preserved Roman theater still used for concerts, discovered by chance after a landslide in the 1970s, sitting right in the middle of one of Europe's oldest continuously inhabited cities. The surrounding Old Town's cobbled lanes are lined with colorful 19th-century Bulgarian Revival houses. Plovdiv's low-key charm and layered history have made it an increasingly popular alternative base to Sofia.
Sofia's city center
A compact capital where a Roman amphitheater sits beneath a modern shopping mall's glass floor, and the golden domes of Alexander Nevsky Cathedral rise a short walk from Ottoman mosques and Soviet-era monuments. Vitosha Mountain looms at the edge of the city, close enough for a half-day hike without leaving urban transit behind. It's a capital that wears its layered, complicated history openly rather than hiding it.
Nessebar Old Town
A UNESCO-listed ancient town on a narrow peninsula on the Black Sea coast, its cobbled streets packed with wooden-balconied houses and more than a dozen medieval churches in various states of ruin. It sits right next to Sunny Beach, Bulgaria's largest and liveliest coastal resort, making the pairing of history and beach time unusually convenient. Sunset over the harbor is a local ritual worth timing a visit around.
Seven Rila Lakes
A cluster of glacial lakes at different elevations in the Rila mountains, connected by hiking trails and best seen from a viewpoint that captures several lakes in a single sweeping shot. A chairlift cuts out much of the climbing, leaving a manageable day hike to the top lakes and back. It's one of Bulgaria's most photographed natural landscapes, especially striking in early autumn.
Rose Valley
A region between the Balkan and Sredna Gora mountains that produces a large share of the world's rose oil, harvested by hand at dawn during a narrow window each May and June. The town of Kazanlak hosts an annual Rose Festival with parades and rose-picking demonstrations timed to the harvest. Outside the festival window, the valley is still a scenic, unhurried stretch of countryside worth a detour.

Suggested itinerary

7 days

7 days covers Sofia, Rila, Plovdiv, and the coast without rushing. 3–4 days works for Sofia and Rila alone; 10 lets you add the Seven Rila Lakes hike or more coastal time.

1. Days 1–2
Sofia
The capital's Roman ruins, golden-domed cathedral, and an easy Vitosha Mountain hike.
Sofia Airport is ~15 min from the city center
Where to eat: Try shopska salad and banitsa (cheese pastry), Bulgaria's everyday staples.
2. Day 3
Rila Monastery
A day trip to Bulgaria's most important Orthodox monastery.
~2h drive from Sofia
Where to eat: Try kavarma, a slow-cooked meat and vegetable stew, at a restaurant near the monastery.
3. Days 4–5
Plovdiv
The Roman theater, Old Town Revival houses, and a relaxed second-city pace.
~2h drive or train from Sofia
Where to eat: Try mekitsa, fried dough served with jam or cheese, a classic Plovdiv breakfast.
4. Days 6–7
Nessebar & the Black Sea coast
A UNESCO old town and beach time to close the trip.
~4h drive from Plovdiv, or a short domestic flight to Burgas
Where to eat: Try fresh grilled fish at a harborside restaurant in Nessebar.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a visa for Bulgaria?
Not for EU citizens, and not for UK, US, and many other passport holders for short tourist stays.
Which airport should I fly into?
Sofia Airport (SOF) for most itineraries, or Burgas (BOJ) for a coast-focused summer trip.
Is Bulgaria part of the euro zone?
Not yet at the time of writing — Bulgaria uses the lev, though eurozone entry has been discussed; check the current status before budgeting.
Is Bulgaria a budget-friendly destination?
Yes — it's consistently one of the cheapest countries in the EU to visit, with prices for food, transport, and lodging well below Western Europe.
