Best time to visit

Saudi Arabia only opened to leisure tourism in 2019, and it's spent the years since unveiling a Nabataean city carved from rose-red rock, a Red Sea coastline of untouched reefs, and a capital reinventing itself in real time.

Tastes of Saudi Arabia

Kabsa
Saudi Arabia's national dish: spiced rice cooked with meat, usually chicken or lamb, and dried fruit or nuts.
Mutabbaq
A stuffed, pan-fried pastry filled with spiced meat, egg, and onion, folded like an envelope.
Jareesh
A porridge-like dish of cracked wheat cooked with meat and spices, a traditional Najd region staple.
Arabic coffee (qahwa)
Lightly roasted, cardamom-spiced coffee served in small cups as a central gesture of Saudi hospitality.

Top attractions

Hegra (Mada'in Salih), AlUla
Saudi Arabia's first UNESCO World Heritage Site, a sprawling Nabataean city of over a hundred monumental tombs carved directly into sandstone outcrops, built by the same civilization behind Jordan's Petra. Unlike Petra, Hegra receives a fraction of the visitors, and its tombs, spread across open desert rather than a single canyon, can often be explored in near-solitude. It sits within AlUla, a region Saudi Arabia has made a centerpiece of its push into leisure tourism.
AlUla Old Town & Elephant Rock
A mudbrick old town abandoned in the 1980s, its maze of narrow, roofed alleys now open for wandering beneath restored facades, with a hilltop fort offering valley views. Nearby, Elephant Rock, a towering sandstone formation shaped uncannily like its namesake, draws sunset crowds to a purpose-built desert viewing area with food trucks. AlUla's wider landscape of canyons and rock art makes it one of the Middle East's most rewarding new destinations.
Edge of the World
A dramatic escarpment of sheer cliffs dropping away toward a vast desert plain, a two-hour drive from Riyadh and reachable partly by 4x4 across open desert. The name comes from the sensation of standing at the cliff edge with nothing but flat horizon stretching endlessly beyond. It's become one of the most popular day trips from the capital since Saudi Arabia opened to tourism, best timed for sunset.
Diriyah
The birthplace of the Saudi state, a mudbrick town on Riyadh's outskirts that served as the first Saudi capital in the 18th century before being destroyed and later restored. At-Turaif, its UNESCO-listed historic core, is being developed into a major cultural district with museums, restaurants, and galleries alongside the restored mudbrick architecture. It offers a direct, walkable look at Saudi history within easy reach of the modern capital.
Red Sea coast diving
Saudi Arabia's Red Sea coastline holds coral reefs left largely undisturbed by mass tourism, in contrast to more developed diving destinations elsewhere on the same sea. Jeddah serves as the main gateway for both diving trips and the historic Al-Balad old town nearby, itself a UNESCO-listed coral-stone merchant quarter. New dedicated dive resorts have begun opening along the coast as the country builds out its tourism infrastructure.
Riyadh's Kingdom Centre & Masmak Fortress
Riyadh pairs a rapidly rising modern skyline, anchored by the Kingdom Centre Tower's distinctive arched top with a public skybridge, against the mudbrick Masmak Fortress downtown, the site of the 1902 raid that began the modern Saudi state's founding. The National Museum nearby fills in the fuller historical picture. Together they capture a capital moving quickly between its past and a very deliberately engineered future.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a visa for Saudi Arabia?
An e-Visa, applied for online, is available for many nationalities and has been generally straightforward since the country opened to tourism in 2019.
Which airport should I fly into?
Riyadh's King Khalid International (RUH) or Jeddah's King Abdulaziz International (JED), depending on whether you start inland or on the coast.
Is there a dress code for visitors?
Modest dress is expected in public for all visitors; it's worth researching current specific guidance for women and men before the trip, as expectations continue to evolve.
Is Saudi Arabia really open to tourists now?
Yes — leisure tourism opened in 2019 with the introduction of the e-Visa, and the country has been actively developing sites like AlUla specifically for international visitors since.
