Nigeria is Africa's most populous country and its cultural engine room — Lagos's Afrobeats scene and Nollywood film industry reach further than almost anywhere else on the continent, backed by ancient kingdoms and wildlife-rich national parks.

Tastes of Nigeria

Jollof rice
A one-pot rice dish cooked in a rich tomato and pepper sauce, a beloved staple across West Africa and a point of friendly national pride.
Suya
Skewered meat coated in a spiced peanut rub and grilled over open flame, a favorite Nigerian street food.
Pepper soup
A light, intensely spiced broth usually made with fish, goat, or chicken.
Pounded yam & egusi soup
Smooth pounded yam served with a rich soup made from ground melon seeds, vegetables, and meat or fish.

Top attractions

Lagos music & nightlife
The birthplace and beating heart of Afrobeats, a genre that has moved from Lagos clubs to global charts over the past decade, with live venues across the city hosting everything from established stars to the next breakout act. Afro-fusion restaurants and rooftop bars in areas like Victoria Island and Lekki keep the energy going well past midnight most nights. It's a city best experienced by following the music rather than a checklist of sights.
Nollywood film industry
Nigeria's film industry, by some measures the second-largest in the world by output, churns out thousands of films a year and has become a genuine cultural export across Africa and its diaspora. Lagos hosts studio tours, film festivals, and screening events for visitors interested in how an industry this prolific actually operates day to day. It's a distinctly Nigerian counterpart to Lagos's music scene, and equally central to understanding the city's cultural weight.
Benin City & the Royal Palace
The historic seat of the centuries-old Kingdom of Benin, once home to an elaborate royal court famous for its bronze and brass plaques, many of which were looted by British forces in 1897 and remain a subject of international restitution efforts today. The current Oba's palace and city museum preserve what remains alongside ongoing efforts to reconstruct the tradition. It offers a very different, older layer of Nigerian history than Lagos's contemporary energy.
Yankari National Park
Nigeria's most visited wildlife reserve, home to elephants, baboons, and one of West Africa's largest remaining elephant populations, alongside the warm, mineral-rich Wikki Warm Spring where visitors can swim year-round. It sits in the country's northeast, requiring a longer overland journey from Lagos or Abuja. It's one of the most reliable places in West Africa to combine a genuine safari experience with a swim.
Abuja
Nigeria's purpose-built capital since 1991, a planned city of wide boulevards and modern architecture in the country's geographic center, in deliberate contrast to Lagos's dense, organic sprawl. Aso Rock, a massive monolith looming over the presidential complex, and the Nigerian National Mosque and National Christian Centre standing near each other are among its most recognizable sights. It's generally calmer and easier to navigate than Lagos, functioning as a useful stopover for onward domestic travel.
Osun-Osogbo Sacred Grove
A UNESCO-listed forest sanctuary along the Osun River, dense with shrines and sculptures dedicated to Osun, the Yoruba goddess of fertility and the river itself, still an active site of worship today. It hosts an annual festival each August drawing worshippers and visitors from across Nigeria and the Yoruba diaspora worldwide. The grove offers a quiet, forested counterpoint to Nigeria's urban energy and a direct window into living Yoruba spiritual tradition.

Suggested itinerary

8 days

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a visa for Nigeria?
Most nationalities need a visa, increasingly available as an e-Visa applied for online — apply with a comfortable buffer before your flight.
Which airport should I fly into?
Lagos's Murtala Muhammed International (LOS) for most itineraries, or Abuja's Nnamdi Azikiwe International (ABV) if starting from the capital.
Is Nigeria safe to visit?
Safety varies significantly by region — Lagos and Abuja's main areas are generally manageable for well-prepared travelers, but check current travel advisories for your specific route, especially the northeast, before finalizing plans.
What's the best way to experience Lagos's music scene?
Ask locally or check listings for live venues in Victoria Island, Lekki, and Ikeja — the scene moves fast and the best nights are often word-of-mouth rather than fixed tourist attractions.
