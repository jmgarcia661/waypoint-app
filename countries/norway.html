Best time to visit

Norway's coastline is defined by its fjords, deep glacial valleys now filled with sea water and framed by cliffs and waterfalls, best seen by a combination of ferry, train, and hiking.

Tastes of Norway

Fårikål
A slow-cooked lamb and cabbage stew, considered Norway's national dish.
Salmon
Fresh or cured Norwegian salmon, a staple of the country's seafood-heavy diet.
Brunost
A caramel-colored, slightly sweet brown cheese, usually sliced thin over bread.
Cloudberries
A golden Nordic berry, often served with cream as a traditional dessert.

Top attractions

Geirangerfjord
A UNESCO-listed fjord ringed by steep cliffs and waterfalls, including the Seven Sisters, which tumble down the rock face directly into the water. A ferry cruise through the fjord is the classic way to see it, passing abandoned farms once perched precariously on the cliffsides. The Trollstigen mountain road nearby adds a dramatic approach by land.
Preikestolen (Pulpit Rock)
A flat-topped cliff rising 604 meters directly above Lysefjord, reached by a moderate but rewarding day hike from a nearby car park. The sheer drop below has made it one of Norway's most photographed viewpoints, though there's no railing at the edge. Good weather makes a significant difference to the experience, so flexible scheduling helps.
Bergen & the Bryggen wharf
Norway's second city sits at the gateway to the western fjords, its old Hanseatic wharf of colorful wooden buildings now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. A funicular up Mount Fløyen offers wide views over the city and surrounding islands. The city's fish market and narrow lanes make it an easy place to spend a full day.
Lofoten Islands
A dramatic archipelago of jagged peaks rising directly from the sea, dotted with traditional red fishing cabins called rorbuer. It's known for both summer's midnight sun and winter's chance at the northern lights, depending on when you visit. Hiking trails here climb steeply for outsized views relative to the effort.
Flåm Railway
One of the steepest standard-gauge railways in the world, descending through mountains and past waterfalls between the high plateau at Myrdal and the fjord village of Flåm. The hour-long ride passes through 20 tunnels and offers a stop at the thundering Kjosfossen waterfall. It's often combined with a fjord cruise to make the popular 'Norway in a Nutshell' route.
Oslo's Vigeland Park
An open-air sculpture park containing over 200 bronze, granite, and iron works by Gustav Vigeland, depicting the human life cycle in often striking, emotional detail. It's part of the larger Frogner Park and remains free and open year-round. Oslo's Viking Ship Museum and waterfront Opera House make natural additions to a city day.

Suggested itinerary

9 days

9 days covers Oslo, Bergen, the Flåm/fjords region, and Stavanger without rushing. 5–6 works if you drop one region; 12+ lets you add the Lofoten Islands properly.

1. Days 1–2
Oslo
Vigeland Park, the Viking Ship Museum, and the harborside Opera House.
Cost: Vigeland Park is free; the Viking Ship Museum costs about NOK 150.
Where to eat: Try fresh shrimp from a stall at Oslo's harbor, eaten straight from the bag, a genuine local tradition.
2. Days 3–4
Bergen
The Bryggen wharf, Mount Fløyen, and the city's fish market.
~7h scenic train from Oslo
Cost: The Fløibanen funicular round trip costs about NOK 180.
Where to eat: Fresh fish soup at Bergen's historic fish market is a reliable, warming choice.
3. Days 5–6
Flåm & the fjords
The Flåm Railway and a cruise through Geirangerfjord or Nærøyfjord.
~3h by train and ferry from Bergen
Cost: The Flåm Railway costs about NOK 560 one-way; a fjord cruise adds NOK 700–900.
Where to eat: Pack your own lunch for the train — no dining car, and the scenery is the real point.
4. Days 7–9
Stavanger & Preikestolen
The Pulpit Rock hike, with time to explore Stavanger's old town.
~4h by ferry and bus from Flåm
Cost: The Preikestolen hike itself is free; parking at the trailhead costs about NOK 250/day.
Where to eat: Refuel after the hike with a hearty meal in Stavanger's old town — you'll have earned it.

Frequently asked questions

How long can I stay in Norway without a visa?
90 days within any 180-day period for US, UK, Canadian, and Australian passport holders — Norway follows the Schengen rule despite not being an EU member. EES biometric registration is now active at the border.
Which airport should I fly into?
Oslo Gardermoen (OSL), the country's main international gateway.
Is the Norway in a Nutshell route worth doing?
Yes — the combination of the Flåm Railway and a fjord cruise packs some of Norway's best scenery into a single, well-organized day.
Is Norway expensive to visit?
Yes, consistently one of the priciest countries in Europe — self-catering and buying alcohol from state-run shops (Vinmonopolet) rather than restaurants helps manage costs.
