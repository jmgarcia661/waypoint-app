Malaysia splits naturally between Kuala Lumpur's modern skyline, the colonial streets and food of Penang, and the rainforest and reefs of Borneo further afield.

Tastes of Malaysia

Nasi lemak
Coconut rice served with sambal, fried anchovies, peanuts, and egg — considered the national dish.
Char kway teow
Stir-fried flat rice noodles with prawns, egg, and bean sprouts, a Penang specialty.
Laksa
A spicy noodle soup with regional variations ranging from curry-based to tangy tamarind broths.
Roti canai
A flaky flatbread served with curry sauce for dipping, popular any time of day.

Top attractions

Petronas Towers
Once the tallest buildings in the world, these twin towers remain Kuala Lumpur's defining landmark, connected by a skybridge on the 41st and 42nd floors. An observation deck near the top offers wide city views, best booked in advance given limited daily tickets. The surrounding KLCC park and mall make it easy to spend a full afternoon in the area.
Batu Caves
A limestone hill riddled with cave temples dedicated to the Hindu god Murugan, reached by climbing 272 brightly painted steps guarded by a towering golden statue at the base. The main cave functions as an active place of worship, especially during the Thaipusam festival. It's a short trip from central Kuala Lumpur.
George Town, Penang
A UNESCO-listed old port city known for its mix of Chinese, Malay, Indian, and colonial British architecture within a compact, walkable core. Street art, working clan jetties, and some of the country's best hawker food make it a favorite stop for a slower few days. Its food scene alone draws repeat visitors.
Cameron Highlands
A cool mountain plateau covered in rolling tea plantations, strawberry farms, and patches of mossy forest, a sharp contrast to the tropical heat elsewhere in the country. Guided jungle walks pass through the Mossy Forest's stunted, lichen-draped trees. Tea estate tours and tastings are the main draw for most visitors.
Kinabalu National Park, Borneo
Home to Mount Kinabalu, Southeast Asia's tallest peak outside the Himalayas, climbable over two days by most reasonably fit hikers with a permit and guide. The surrounding park also protects extraordinary biodiversity, including carnivorous pitcher plants found nowhere else. It's usually paired with a trip to see orangutans elsewhere in Sabah.
Semenggoh Wildlife Centre
A rehabilitation center in Sarawak, Borneo, where semi-wild orangutans that were once orphaned or injured return during scheduled feeding times. Sightings aren't guaranteed since the orangutans forage freely in surrounding forest, which adds to the appeal of seeing them in a genuinely wild-adjacent setting. It's one of the more accessible ways to see orangutans in Malaysian Borneo.

Suggested itinerary

10 days

10 days covers Kuala Lumpur, the Cameron Highlands, Penang, and Borneo without rushing. 5–6 works for just the peninsula; 12–14 lets you slow the Borneo leg down considerably.

1. Days 1–3
Kuala Lumpur
The Petronas Towers, Batu Caves, and the city's varied food scene.
Cost: Petronas Towers Skybridge and observation deck tickets cost about MYR 98; Batu Caves entry is free.
Where to eat: Try nasi lemak from a street stall for breakfast — genuinely eaten that way by locals.
2. Day 4
Cameron Highlands
Tea plantations and a cool-climate break from the tropical lowlands.
~3.5h drive from Kuala Lumpur
Cost: Tea plantation tours cost about MYR 5–15 for basic entry.
Where to eat: Try a pot of fresh Cameron Highlands tea with scones — a colonial-era holdover that's become a genuine local specialty.
3. Days 5–6
Penang
George Town's old streets, street art, and hawker food.
~3h drive from Cameron Highlands
Cost: Most of George Town's street art and old streets are free; a hawker meal costs MYR 8–15.
Where to eat: Char kway teow from a well-reviewed hawker stall is the Penang dish to prioritize.
4. Days 7–10
Borneo (Sabah/Sarawak)
Orangutan sightings and, for the more ambitious, a climb of Mount Kinabalu.
~2.5h flight from Penang
Cost: A guided orangutan sanctuary visit costs about MYR 30–50; a river cruise for wildlife spotting runs MYR 150–300.
Where to eat: Try Sarawak laksa, distinct from the Peninsula versions, if visiting that side of Borneo.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a visa for Malaysia?
Not for most Western passport holders for short tourist stays.
Which airport should I fly into?
Kuala Lumpur International (KUL), the country's main gateway.
Do I need to book orangutan viewing in Borneo in advance?
Recommended for the better-known sanctuaries and river cruises, especially in peak season, though some centers accept walk-ins.
Is Malaysia expensive to visit?
No, genuinely affordable — one of the better values in Southeast Asia for food, transport, and accommodation.
