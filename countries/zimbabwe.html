Tastes of Zimbabwe

Sadza
A stiff maize porridge and the country's staple food, usually eaten with a meat or vegetable relish.
Mopane worms
Dried, protein-rich caterpillars, a traditional delicacy across the region.
Bota
A smooth, sweetened maize porridge typically eaten for breakfast.
Chibuku
A traditional fermented sorghum or maize beer, sold and enjoyed widely across the country.

Top attractions

Victoria Falls
Known locally as Mosi-oa-Tunya, 'the smoke that thunders', this is one of the largest waterfalls in the world by combined width and height. The Zimbabwean side offers the widest, most direct views of the main falls, especially in high water season when spray can be seen for kilometers. Bridge walks and adventure activities like bungee jumping add to the appeal.
Hwange National Park
Zimbabwe's largest park holds one of Africa's biggest elephant populations, along with strong lion and wild dog numbers. Waterhole-based game viewing is particularly good in the dry season, when animals concentrate around a limited number of pumped water points. It sees far fewer visitors than comparable parks elsewhere in the region.
Mana Pools National Park
A UNESCO-listed floodplain along the Zambezi River, known for allowing walking safaris in the presence of large game, a rarity in African parks. Elephants, buffalo, and predators gather along the river's pools during the dry season. Its remoteness and strict visitor limits keep it feeling genuinely wild.
Great Zimbabwe ruins
The stone remains of a medieval city that once anchored a major trading empire, built entirely without mortar from carefully cut granite blocks. It's the largest ancient stone structure in sub-Saharan Africa and gave the country its name. The site includes a hilltop complex and a lower enclosure with massive curved walls.
Lake Kariba
One of the largest man-made lakes in the world, formed by damming the Zambezi River, now ringed by wildlife-rich shoreline and houseboats. Sunset cruises here are known for close elephant and buffalo sightings as animals come down to drink. It's a relaxed contrast to the more intense game drives elsewhere.
Matobo Hills
A landscape of huge balancing granite boulders scattered across rolling hills, sacred to local Ndebele and Shona communities and home to ancient rock art. It also holds one of the country's best rhino tracking experiences, done on foot with a guide. Cecil Rhodes chose to be buried here, at a site called World's View.

Suggested itinerary

8 days

8 days covers Victoria Falls, Hwange, Matobo Hills, and Great Zimbabwe at a solid pace. 5–6 works if you drop one park; 10+ lets you slow the safari legs down.

1. Days 1–2
Victoria Falls
The falls themselves, plus optional adventure activities like the bridge walk or a sunset cruise.
Cost: Zimbabwean-side park entry costs about $30 for foreigners; the bridge walk or bungee adds $50–160.
Where to eat: Try a proper Zimbabwean sadza dinner at a local restaurant in Victoria Falls town, away from the resort strip.
2. Days 3–5
Hwange National Park
Waterhole-based game drives through Zimbabwe's largest wildlife reserve.
~1.5h drive from Victoria Falls
Cost: Bundled into lodge packages, roughly $300–600+/night all-inclusive.
Where to eat: Meals are included at nearly every Hwange lodge, often built around the day's game-drive schedule.
3. Days 6–7
Matobo Hills
Rhino tracking on foot and rock art among giant granite boulders.
~4h drive from Hwange, via Bulawayo
Cost: A guided rhino tracking walk costs about $50–70 per person.
Where to eat: Pack a picnic for the day — options inside Matobo itself are minimal.
4. Day 8
Great Zimbabwe
A stop at the ancient stone ruins before departure from Harare.
~3h drive from Matobo Hills
Cost: Site entry costs about $10–15 for foreigners.
Where to eat: A simple meal in nearby Masvingo is the practical choice before continuing to Harare.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a visa for Zimbabwe?
Yes, a visa on arrival is available for most nationalities, including the KAZA Univisa, which also covers Zambia.
Which airport should I fly into?
Victoria Falls Airport (VFA), right by the falls and the natural start for this route.
Which side of Victoria Falls is better, Zimbabwe or Zambia?
Zimbabwe generally offers more expansive, dramatic views of the main falls face; Zambia gets you closer to the water, including swimming at Devil's Pool in the dry season. The KAZA Univisa lets you see both.
Is Zimbabwe safe to visit?
Yes, tourist areas around Victoria Falls and the main parks are well set up and safe for visitors; normal precautions apply as anywhere.
