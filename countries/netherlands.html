Best time to visit

Amsterdam's canal-ringed old center, combined with the country's flat, easy cycling terrain, makes the Netherlands one of the simplest countries in Europe to explore slowly and on two wheels.

Top attractions

Amsterdam's canal ring
A UNESCO-listed network of concentric canals lined with narrow, gabled 17th-century townhouses, best seen either on foot or from a canal boat gliding beneath the arched bridges. The canal houses' distinctive lean and hooks above the top windows, once used for hoisting furniture, are a small architectural quirk worth noticing. Cycling along the canal paths remains one of the most authentic ways to experience the city.
Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam
The Netherlands' national museum, holding an extensive collection of Dutch Golden Age painting including Rembrandt's The Night Watch and Vermeer's The Milkmaid. Its restored 19th-century building is itself a work of art, with elaborate ceiling paintings throughout the galleries. A single visit typically requires several hours to see the highlights properly.
Keukenhof Gardens
One of the largest flower gardens in the world, open only during a roughly eight-week window each spring when millions of tulips, daffodils, and hyacinths bloom in elaborate designed beds. The surrounding countryside is also lined with commercial tulip fields worth a separate detour by bike or car. Its short opening season means planning ahead is essential.
Anne Frank House
The canal house where Anne Frank and her family hid for two years during the Nazi occupation, now preserved largely as it was, including the movable bookcase that concealed the entrance to their hiding place. Anne's original diary pages are displayed alongside photographs and family artifacts. Tickets sell out well in advance and must be booked online.
Zaanse Schans windmills
A collection of historic Dutch windmills and green wooden houses relocated to form an open-air preservation of traditional Dutch village life. Several windmills remain functional, still grinding spices, oil, or pigments as demonstrations for visitors. It's an easy half-day trip from Amsterdam for the classic windmill image many associate with the country.
Kinderdijk
Nineteen well-preserved 18th-century windmills line the waterways of this UNESCO World Heritage Site, built as part of an elaborate water management system to keep the low-lying land dry. Cycling or walking the paths between the windmills offers an uncluttered view rarely seen at more commercialized sites. It's a quieter alternative to Zaanse Schans, especially outside peak hours.

Suggested itinerary

6 days

6 days covers Amsterdam and easy day trips to the windmills and tulip fields without rushing. 3–4 works for just Amsterdam; 8–9 lets you add Rotterdam or The Hague.

1. Days 1–3
Amsterdam
The canal ring, Rijksmuseum, and the Anne Frank House.
Book ahead
Cost: The Anne Frank House costs about €16 and must be booked online in advance; canal cruises run €18–20.
Where to eat: Try a fresh stroopwafel from a market stall — best eaten warm, right off the griddle.
2. Day 4
Keukenhof (spring only) or Zaanse Schans
Either the seasonal tulip gardens or the windmill village, depending on timing.
~20min drive from Amsterdam
Cost: Keukenhof entry costs about €21 (seasonal, spring only); Zaanse Schans is free to walk, with individual windmill tours costing a few euros each.
Where to eat: Try poffertjes, small fluffy Dutch pancakes, sold at stalls in both locations.
3. Days 5–6
Kinderdijk & departure
A quieter windmill site before returning to Amsterdam for departure.
~1h drive from Amsterdam
Cost: Kinderdijk entry to the windmill area costs about €12.
Where to eat: A final stroopwafel or bitterballen at Schiphol's food hall is a fitting last Dutch bite before departure.

Frequently asked questions

How long can I stay in the Netherlands without a visa?
90 days within any 180-day period for US, UK, Canadian, and Australian passport holders — the standard Schengen rule. EES biometric registration is now active at the border.
Which airport should I fly into?
Amsterdam Schiphol (AMS), one of Europe's best-connected airports and a short train ride from the city center.
Do I need to book the Anne Frank House in advance?
Yes, essential — tickets are released online weeks ahead and sell out quickly. Don't expect to walk up.
When's the best time to see the tulip fields?
Roughly mid-March to mid-May, with peak bloom usually in April. Keukenhof's opening dates shift slightly each year based on the season.
