Tastes of Serbia

Ćevapi
Grilled minced-meat sausages, a beloved staple shared across the Balkans.
Ajvar
A roasted red pepper and eggplant relish, spread on bread or served as a side.
Sarma
Cabbage rolls stuffed with meat and rice, a hearty winter classic.
Rakija
A strong fruit brandy, often homemade, and central to Serbian hospitality.

Top attractions

Belgrade Fortress & Kalemegdan Park
A fortress dating back to Roman times, sitting at the confluence of the Sava and Danube rivers, its layered ruins reflecting centuries of Ottoman, Austrian, and Serbian control. The surrounding Kalemegdan Park is one of the city's most popular gathering spots, especially at sunset over the river junction below. A military museum within the fortress walls covers the region's long military history.
Skadarlija, Belgrade
A cobbled bohemian quarter modeled loosely on Montmartre, lined with traditional restaurants serving Serbian cuisine alongside live folk music most evenings. It was once home to many of Belgrade's writers and artists in the early 20th century. It remains one of the liveliest areas of the city after dark.
Studenica Monastery
A UNESCO-listed medieval monastery founded in the 12th century, its white marble churches containing some of the best-preserved Byzantine fresco painting in the Balkans. It remains an active monastic community set in a quiet forested valley in central Serbia. The site holds deep significance in Serbian Orthodox religious and national identity.
Đavolja Varoš (Devil's Town)
A cluster of over 200 unusual earth pyramids, formed by erosion, each capped with a boulder that protects the softer soil beneath from washing away. Local legend attributes the eerie formations to petrified wedding guests cursed by the devil. The site is one of Serbia's more unusual natural attractions, tucked into the country's south.
Novi Sad & Petrovaradin Fortress
An 18th-century Austro-Hungarian fortress overlooking the Danube in Novi Sad, Serbia's second city, its clock tower famously showing the hour and minute hands reversed in size. The fortress hosts the EXIT music festival each summer, drawing large international crowds. The old town below retains a distinctly Central European feel compared to Belgrade.
Uvac Canyon
A dramatically winding river canyon in southwestern Serbia, its tight meanders best appreciated from a series of clifftop viewpoints. It's also an important refuge for the griffon vulture, one of Europe's largest birds of prey. Boat trips through the canyon are available for a closer view of the winding gorge.

Suggested itinerary

6 days

6 days covers Belgrade, Novi Sad, Uvac Canyon, and Studenica without rushing. 3–4 works for just Belgrade and Novi Sad; 8+ lets you add more of the south's monasteries.

1. Days 1–2
Belgrade
The fortress, Kalemegdan Park, and an evening in the Skadarlija quarter.
Cost: Kalemegdan Fortress and park are free to explore; a night out in Skadarlija costs the price of dinner and drinks.
Where to eat: Try ćevapi at a traditional kafana in Skadarlija, alongside live Balkan music.
2. Day 3
Novi Sad
Petrovaradin Fortress and the more Central European old town.
~1h drive from Belgrade
Cost: Petrovaradin Fortress is free to explore.
Where to eat: Try ajvar spread on fresh bread as a simple, genuine local snack.
3. Day 4
Uvac Canyon
Clifftop viewpoints over the winding river gorge.
~3h drive from Novi Sad
Cost: Guided boat tours on the reservoir cost about €15–25 per person.
Where to eat: Pack a picnic — this is a remote, scenic stop with limited dining options.
4. Days 5–6
Studenica & the south
The medieval monastery and, time permitting, Devil's Town's earth pyramids.
~1.5h drive from Uvac Canyon
Cost: Monastery entry is typically free, though donations are welcomed.
Where to eat: A simple meal in a nearby village restaurant suits the reflective pace of this stop.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a visa for Serbia?
Not for most Western passport holders (EU, UK, US, Canada) for short stays.
Which airport should I fly into?
Belgrade Nikola Tesla Airport (BEG), the country's main gateway.
Is Belgrade's nightlife really as good as people say?
Yes, genuinely — the city's river-barge clubs (splavovi) along the Sava and Danube are a distinctive, well-known part of Belgrade's social scene.
Is Serbia expensive to visit?
No, one of the better values in the Balkans for food, transport, and accommodation.
