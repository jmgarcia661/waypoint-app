Perched on Mount Titano and surrounded entirely by Italy, San Marino claims to be the world's oldest surviving republic, its medieval towers reachable as an easy day trip from the Adriatic coast.

Top attractions

Three Towers of San Marino
Three medieval fortresses, Guaita, Cesta, and Montale, sit along the ridge of Mount Titano, each built at a different point to defend the republic and offering increasingly dramatic views as visitors move between them. Guaita, the oldest and most visited, dates to the 11th century and remains partly used for ceremonial purposes. On a clear day, the view extends across the Adriatic coastline below.
Basilica di San Marino
A neoclassical basilica built in the 19th century on the site of an earlier church, said to house the remains of Saint Marinus, the stonemason credited with founding the republic in the 4th century. Its columned facade and central position in the old town make it a natural stop on any walk through the capital. The interior is more restrained than the exterior might suggest.
Palazzo Pubblico
San Marino's town hall and seat of government, its crenellated tower and clock face overlooking the main square. A changing of the guard ceremony takes place several times a day, drawing small crowds of visitors. The building's neo-Gothic style, rebuilt in the late 19th century, deliberately evokes the republic's medieval origins.
State Museum of San Marino
Housed in a former convent, this museum traces the republic's long history through archaeological finds, paintings, and artifacts spanning from prehistory to the modern era. It offers useful context for a country whose independence dates back further than most European nations. The collection is compact enough to see in under an hour.
Cable car to Borgo Maggiore
A short cable car connects the hilltop capital to Borgo Maggiore below, a market town with its own more relaxed pace and a large Thursday market. The ride itself offers sweeping views over the surrounding Italian countryside. It's a straightforward way to see more of the country beyond the walled capital.
City walls and gates
The old capital remains largely enclosed by its original medieval walls, entered through a series of stone gates that have changed little in appearance for centuries. Walking the perimeter offers a strong sense of the city's defensive layout and its commanding position atop Mount Titano. The narrow lanes inside are largely pedestrian and easy to explore on foot.

Suggested itinerary

2 days

2 days covers the Three Towers and the old town comfortably — San Marino is small enough to see thoroughly in this time, best combined with a longer Italy trip through Emilia-Romagna or the Marche.

1. Day 1
The Three Towers
Walk the ridge of Mount Titano between Guaita, Cesta, and Montale for coastal views.
Cost: A combined ticket for the two accessible towers (Guaita and Cesta) costs about €10.
Where to eat: Try piadina from a stand along the walk — a quick, satisfying regional staple.
2. Day 2
Old town & Borgo Maggiore
The Basilica, Palazzo Pubblico, and a cable car ride down to the market town below.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a visa for San Marino?
Not if you can enter Italy or the Schengen Area — San Marino follows similar rules.
How do I get to San Marino?
Fly into Bologna (BLQ) or Rimini (RMI), then drive or take a bus — Rimini is closer, about 45 minutes away.
Is San Marino part of the EU?
No — it's an independent microstate, though closely tied to Italy and using the euro.
Is San Marino worth a dedicated day trip?
Yes, especially for the walk along Mount Titano's ridge between the three towers — genuinely dramatic views over the surrounding Italian countryside.
