Top attractions

Tower of London
A nearly thousand-year-old fortress on the Thames that has served as a royal palace, prison, and treasury, now home to the Crown Jewels. Yeoman Warders, known as Beefeaters, lead tours covering centuries of executions, escapes, and royal history within its walls. Its ravens, protected by legend and tradition, are as much a fixture as the stonework.
Stonehenge
A prehistoric circle of massive standing stones on Salisbury Plain, erected in stages over thousands of years for purposes still debated by archaeologists. Its alignment with the summer and winter solstices suggests some astronomical or ceremonial function, though certainty remains elusive. A visitor center and surrounding barrows add context to the stones themselves.
Edinburgh Castle
Perched on an extinct volcanic crag above Edinburgh's old town, this castle has anchored Scottish history for centuries, housing the Scottish crown jewels and the Stone of Destiny used in royal coronations. A cannon still fires each day at one o'clock, a tradition dating back to give ships an accurate time signal. The Royal Mile leads directly from its gates through the heart of the old city.
British Museum, London
A vast, free museum holding artifacts spanning human history from nearly every continent, including the Rosetta Stone and the Parthenon Marbles. Its glass-roofed Great Court, one of the largest covered public squares in Europe, sits at the heart of the building. A single visit can only meaningfully cover a small selection of its eight million objects.
Lake District
A national park of glacial lakes and fell walking trails in northwest England, home to England's highest peak, Scafell Pike, and long associated with the Romantic poets, including William Wordsworth. Small stone villages like Ambleside and Grasmere serve as bases for hiking and lakeside walks. It's a favorite for a slower, greener contrast to the country's cities.
Giant's Causeway
Around 40,000 interlocking hexagonal basalt columns line the coast of Northern Ireland, formed by an ancient volcanic eruption but wrapped in local legend involving a giant named Finn McCool. A coastal path leads directly among the columns, with dramatic cliff scenery along the wider Causeway Coast. It's a UNESCO World Heritage Site and Northern Ireland's most visited natural attraction.

Suggested itinerary

11 days

11 days covers London, the Lake District, Edinburgh, and Northern Ireland at a relaxed pace. 5–6 works for just London and one region; 14+ lets you add Wales or the Cotswolds.

1. Days 1–4
London
The Tower of London, the British Museum, and a day trip to Stonehenge.
Cost: The Tower of London costs about £34.80; the British Museum is free (donations welcome).
Where to eat: A pub Sunday roast, even at a mid-range gastropub, is a genuinely worthwhile London meal.
2. Days 5–6
Lake District
Fell walking and lakeside villages in England's northwest.
~4h by train from London
Cost: Fell walking itself is free; a Windermere lake cruise costs about £15–20.
Where to eat: Try a Cumberland sausage at a village pub — a distinctive regional specialty, coiled and peppery.
3. Days 7–8
Edinburgh
Edinburgh Castle and the Royal Mile through Scotland's old town.
~3h drive from the Lake District
Cost: Edinburgh Castle entry costs about £19.50; the Royal Mile itself is free to walk.
Where to eat: A proper haggis, neeps, and tatties dinner is worth trying at least once, ideally at a traditional pub.
4. Days 9–11
Northern Ireland
The Giant's Causeway and the Causeway Coast before departure.
~1h flight from Edinburgh to Belfast
Cost: The Giant's Causeway visitor center costs about £13.50, though the causeway itself is free to walk.
Where to eat: Try an Ulster fry, Northern Ireland's heartier take on the full breakfast, including soda and potato bread.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a visa for the UK?
Not a visa for most Western passport holders, but as of 25 February 2026 you do need a UK ETA (£20, applied for online before travel) — this now applies to EU, US, Canadian, and Australian citizens alike, a recent change many older guides don't mention.
Which airport should I fly into?
London Heathrow (LHR) is the main gateway for this itinerary; Gatwick (LGW) is a common alternative with slightly different transport links.
Do I need a car for this itinerary?
Helpful for the Lake District, but London, Edinburgh, and the inter-city legs are all well covered by train — a car isn't necessary for the whole trip.
Is the UK expensive to visit?
London runs high, especially accommodation. The Lake District, Edinburgh, and Northern Ireland are comparatively better value.
