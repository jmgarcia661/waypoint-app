Best time to visit

Rwanda is one of only three countries on Earth where you can trek to see wild mountain gorillas, set in a small, startlingly clean and orderly country that's remade itself into one of Africa's safest destinations.

Tastes of Rwanda

Brochettes
Grilled meat skewers, usually goat or beef, a popular street food and bar snack across Rwanda.
Isombe
A stew of pounded cassava leaves, often cooked with eggplant and served with rice or ugali.
Ugali
A dense, starchy maize porridge that's a staple accompaniment to most Rwandan meals.
Rwandan coffee
High-altitude arabica grown on the country's terraced hillsides, increasingly recognized among Africa's finest.

Top attractions

Volcanoes National Park gorilla trekking
Rwanda is one of only three countries where wild mountain gorillas can be visited, and Volcanoes National Park, spread across a chain of dormant volcanoes, offers some of the most accessible and well-organized trekking of the three. Groups of up to eight visitors, guided by trackers, spend up to an hour with a habituated gorilla family once located, often just a few meters away. Trekking permits are expensive and limited, and need booking well in advance through Rwanda's tourism board.
Kigali Genocide Memorial
A memorial and museum documenting the 1994 genocide against the Tutsi, built on a site that is also the resting place of over 250,000 victims. Exhibits combine historical documentation with deeply personal testimony and a dedicated children's memorial room. It's a sober, essential stop for understanding modern Rwanda, and central to how the country has approached reconciliation and rebuilding since.
Kigali city
Frequently cited as one of Africa's cleanest and safest capitals, with plastic bags banned nationwide and a mandatory monthly community clean-up day (umuganda) that residents and often visitors join. Kigali's hills give it a green, undulating layout, and the Kimironko Market and a growing contemporary art and coffee-shop scene round out a city that rewards more time than most visitors initially plan for. It also functions as the natural hub for onward travel to the gorillas or Nyungwe Forest.
Nyungwe Forest National Park
One of Africa's oldest and most biodiverse rainforests, home to chimpanzees, thirteen other primate species, and a canopy walkway suspended 70 meters above the forest floor. Chimpanzee trekking here is less crowded and often more affordable than gorilla trekking, making it a popular complement to a Volcanoes National Park visit. The forest also marks the source of the Nile and Congo river systems, a genuine watershed in the most literal sense.
Lake Kivu
One of Africa's Great Lakes, forming much of the border with the Democratic Republic of Congo, ringed by terraced hillsides, coffee plantations, and relaxed lakeside towns like Gisenyi and Kibuye. It's Rwanda's classic post-trekking decompression stop, with boat trips, kayaking, and simply lounging on the shore as the main draws. The lake's methane and CO2 deposits, unique among the world's lakes, are also being developed as a source of clean energy.
Golden monkey trekking
A shorter, more affordable alternative to gorilla trekking within the same Volcanoes National Park, tracking troops of endangered golden monkeys through bamboo forest on the volcanoes' lower slopes. The monkeys are notably more active and playful than gorillas, often seen leaping between bamboo stalks at close range. It's commonly combined with gorilla trekking on a multi-day Volcanoes itinerary for travelers wanting both primate experiences.

Suggested itinerary

6 days

6 days covers Kigali, gorilla trekking, and a lakeside close without rushing. 3–4 days works for Kigali and Volcanoes National Park alone; 8–9 lets you add Nyungwe Forest's chimpanzee trekking in more depth.

1. Days 1–2
Kigali
The Genocide Memorial, city highlights, and acclimatization before heading to the volcanoes.
Kigali International Airport is ~10 min from the city center
Where to eat: Try brochettes (grilled skewers) and isombe (cassava leaf stew), Rwandan staples found across the city.
2. Days 3–4
Volcanoes National Park
Gorilla trekking and, optionally, golden monkey trekking on the volcanoes' slopes.
~2.5h drive from KigaliBook ahead
Cost: A gorilla trekking permit costs USD 1,500 per person; golden monkey permits are significantly cheaper, around USD 100.
3. Days 5–6
Lake Kivu
A relaxed lakeside close to the trip, with boat trips and coffee-country scenery.
~2.5h drive from Volcanoes National Park to Gisenyi on the lakeshore
Where to eat: Try Rwandan coffee, grown on the terraced hills around the lake, at a lakeside cafe.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a visa for Rwanda?
An e-Visa or visa on arrival is available for most nationalities — check current fees and requirements before booking.
Which airport should I fly into?
Kigali International Airport (KGL), the country's main gateway.
How much does gorilla trekking cost?
A permit costs USD 1,500 per person as of recent pricing, booked through Rwanda's tourism board — always confirm the current fee, as it has changed over time.
Do I need to book gorilla trekking far in advance?
Yes — permits are limited to a small number of visitors per gorilla family per day and often sell out months ahead, especially in peak dry season.
