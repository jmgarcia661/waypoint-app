Tastes of Latvia

Grey peas with bacon
A hearty dish of grey peas cooked with smoked bacon and onion, a Latvian winter classic served year-round.
Rupjmaize
Dense, dark Latvian rye bread, a staple at nearly every meal.
Smoked fish
Freshly smoked fish, especially sprat and eel, sold at markets and coastal stalls.
Riga Black Balsam
A bitter, herbal liqueur that's been produced in Riga since the 18th century, often mixed into cocktails.

Top attractions

Art Nouveau District, Riga
Riga holds the largest and most concentrated collection of Art Nouveau buildings in the world, roughly a third of the city center's architecture, with Alberta Street alone home to some of the style's most elaborate facades. Ornate faces, floral motifs, and sweeping curves cover buildings designed by leading architects of the early 20th century, including a young Mikhail Eisenstein. A dedicated museum in a former Art Nouveau apartment shows how these buildings were actually lived in.
Riga Old Town
A UNESCO-listed medieval old town of narrow cobbled streets, guild halls, and the House of the Blackheads, a lavishly restored Gothic merchants' hall on the main square. St. Peter's Church tower offers one of the best panoramic views over the old town's red roofs and the Daugava River. Cafes and craft shops fill the ground floors, giving the historic core a genuinely lived-in, unfussy feel.
Jurmala
A beach resort town stretching along a 20-plus-kilometer strip of fine white sand and pine forest, a short train ride from central Riga and a favorite retreat since the Soviet era. Wooden villas, some beautifully restored and some still crumbling, line the streets behind the dunes. It's easily done as a half-day trip or a relaxed overnight stay away from the city.
Gauja National Park
Latvia's largest national park, a river valley of sandstone cliffs, dense pine forest, and medieval castles at Sigulda and Turaida, often called 'Latvian Switzerland' despite its modest hills. A cable car crosses the Gauja River valley at Sigulda, and the area doubles as a bobsled and toboggan hub in winter. It's an easy day trip from Riga and a common overnight base for hiking.
Riga Central Market
One of Europe's largest and oldest markets, housed partly inside five former German Zeppelin hangars repurposed in the 1930s, selling fresh produce, smoked fish, and Latvian rye bread. It's a genuinely functional, unpolished market rather than a tourist-oriented food hall, giving a direct look at everyday Riga life. The scale of the hangar buildings alone makes it worth a walk-through even without buying anything.
Rundale Palace
A lavish baroque and rococo palace built for the Dukes of Courland, designed by the same architect behind St. Petersburg's Winter Palace, with formal French gardens laid out in front. It sits about 80 kilometers south of Riga, making it a common day trip combined with the town of Bauska nearby. The interior's gilded state rooms are considered among the finest baroque interiors in the Baltics.

Suggested itinerary

5 days

5 days covers Riga, Jurmala, and Gauja National Park at an unhurried pace. 2–3 days works for Riga alone; 7 lets you add Rundale Palace and more of the coast.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a visa for Latvia?
Not for EU citizens, and not for UK, US, and many other passport holders for short Schengen-area stays.
Which airport should I fly into?
Riga International Airport (RIX), the main hub for the Baltic region.
Is Latvia part of the euro zone?
Yes — Latvia uses the euro and is a full Schengen and EU member.
Can I easily combine Latvia with Estonia and Lithuania?
Yes — the three Baltic capitals are well connected by bus, and many travelers combine all three in a single 10–14 day trip.
